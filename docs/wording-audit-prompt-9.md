# Brave Blocks Wording Review — Prompt 9

**Review date:** July 25, 2026  
**Scope:** Home and Quest Map, all 10 child-facing quests, Pause Portal, Voice Lab, rewards and loot, page-read narration, quest narration, and the REVIEW/CHILD meeting cards  
**Review lenses:** trauma therapist, children’s attorney, and early-reading specialist

## Outcome

This fresh-eyes pass found **41 wording items or repeated wording patterns** worth revising. Most are not severe problems. The strongest parts of Brave Blocks remain the respected PASS option, equal XP for passing or completing, repeated permission not to explain, clear statements that the game does not save or send answers, and reminders that the child does not have to guess or choose sides.

The flags fall into four themes:

1. A few lines tell the child what they feel, want, or notice instead of offering a possibility.
2. A few reward lines could be read as rewarding the child’s statement rather than rewarding practice.
3. A few safety, support, privacy, and faith lines sound more certain than the game can guarantee.
4. Several longer or abstract phrases can be made easier for an early first-grade reader without losing the gamer voice.

This document records proposed replacements for caregiver, clinician, attorney, and WRAP-team review. **Prompt 9 did not silently change the live child-facing wording.** Role and privacy wording for the actual attorney and social worker still requires each professional’s approval.

## Screen-by-screen flags

### Home, Quest Map, Voice Lab, and global narration

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Every feeling is allowed—even the giant, messy ones.” | Trauma therapist | “Messy” can accidentally label the child’s inner experience as disorderly or bad. | “Every feeling is allowed—even the giant ones.” |
| “your safe grown-ups are on your team.” | Children’s attorney | The game cannot guarantee that a particular adult is available, safe, or acting as the child expects. | “You can ask a safe grown-up for help.” |
| “No saves. No sending. Your picks stay on this screen.” | Children’s attorney | “Stay on this screen” can sound like a confidentiality promise even though a nearby person can still see the screen. | “The game does not save or send your picks. Someone near you can still see the screen.” |
| “Support squad: always equipped.” | Children’s attorney | “Always” promises support availability that the game cannot verify. | “Your support squad is on your list.” |
| “Brave experience points” | Reading specialist | “Experience” is an abstract, multisyllable word when “XP” is already familiar gamer language. | “Brave points” |
| “This original gaming-adventure narrator is prerecorded, so it sounds the same on the phone, computer, and Fire tablet.” | Reading specialist | “Original,” “gaming-adventure,” and “prerecorded” create a long sentence with several harder words. | “This voice is recorded already. It sounds the same on every device.” |
| “The voice is already inside the game. The player’s taps and choices are never sent to a voice service.” | Reading specialist | “Voice service” is technical and the third-person “player” is less direct. | “The voice is inside the game. The game does not send your taps or picks to a voice app.” |
| “ALL FEELINGS = VALID · NO CAP” | Reading specialist | “Valid” is clinical and less familiar than the rest of the footer. | “ALL FEELINGS ARE OKAY · NO CAP” |

### Quest 1 — Vibe Mixer

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Happy — light + bouncy”; “Sad — heavy + slow”; “Mad — hot + stompy”; “Worried — jumpy + buzzy”; “Scared — shaky + fast”; “Confused — foggy + unsure”; “Loved — warm + safe”; “Mixed — a whole combo” | Trauma therapist | Fixed feeling-to-body pairings can sound like the game is telling the child what each feeling must feel like. | Add possibility language to every clue: “maybe light or bouncy,” “maybe heavy or slow,” “maybe hot or stompy,” “maybe jumpy or buzzy,” “maybe shaky or fast,” “maybe foggy or unsure,” “maybe warm or safe,” and “maybe a whole combo.” |
| “That mix is valid. Huge W.” | Reading specialist | “Valid” is abstract and clinical for an early reader. | “Those feelings can be here. Huge W.” |
| “Opposite feelings can team up. Nothing weird about it.” | Trauma therapist | “Opposite” is abstract, while introducing “weird” may add shame that was not present. | “Different feelings can show up together. That is okay.” |

### Quest 2 — Body Radar

No wording was flagged. The screen offers body locations and sensations as selectable possibilities, includes “something else,” and allows an equal-credit skip. Those choices keep the activity exploratory instead of telling the child what their body means.

### Quest 3 — Dragon Battle

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Slow breath = max shield power.” | Trauma therapist | It frames a particular body response as the winning response, even though breath focus does not help every child. | “Try a slow breath—or skip. Both get the W.” |
| “HOLD” | Trauma therapist | A timed breath hold can be uncomfortable or activating and is not needed for the inhale/exhale animation. | Remove the required hold step; move from “BREATHE IN” to “BLOW OUT WHEN READY.” |

### Quest 4 — Meeting Loadout

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “ATTORNEY. My job is to listen and explain my role. Ask me what I keep private and what I may share.” | Children’s attorney | The app speaks in the attorney’s voice before the actual attorney has approved the role or privacy wording. | “ATTORNEY. Ask: ‘What is your job? What do you keep private? What might you tell someone else?’” **Use only after the child’s attorney approves it.** |
| “SOCIAL WORKER. My job is to check how things are going. Ask me what I write down or share.” | Children’s attorney | The description may not match the worker’s actual duties or required disclosures in this case. | “SOCIAL WORKER. Ask: ‘What is your job? What do you write down? Who might see it?’” **Use only after the child’s social worker approves it.** |
| “No answer earns more points than another.” | Reading specialist | The sentence is important but longer than necessary. | “Every choice gets the same points.” |
| “COMMUNICATION PORTAL” | Reading specialist | “Communication” is a long, abstract word for an early first-grade reader. | “ANSWER PORTAL” |
| “AFTER-MEETING LANDING PAD” | Reading specialist | This compound label asks the child to decode three concepts at once. | “AFTER THE MEETING” |
| “You still get care and connection after any answer—or no answer.” | Children’s attorney | The game cannot guarantee another person’s future care or behavior. | “Any answer—or no answer—gets the same XP here.” |
| “Nothing picked, pointed to, or drawn here is saved or sent.” | Reading specialist | The privacy idea is strong, but the repeated verb pattern is harder to hold in working memory. | “The game does not save or send your picks or drawings.” |

### Quest 5 — Talk Power-Up

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Hi! My job is to listen. How are you feeling?” | Children’s attorney | A fictional professional makes a role promise and immediately asks for emotional content, which can resemble rehearsing an interview. | “Hi. You can talk, point, draw, or pass.” |
| “Your brain feels foggy. What move could help?” | Trauma therapist | It assigns an internal state to the child. | “If words feel hard, what could help?” |
| “You want more time. Pick your power move.” | Trauma therapist | It assigns a preference before the child chooses it. | “Need more time? Pick a move—or pass.” |
| “Nice move. Your own words have power.” | Children’s attorney | Because it follows a selected statement, it can look like the game rewards what the child said. | “Nice move. You chose your own words.” |
| “Ask each grown-up what they will keep private and what they may share.” | Reading specialist | The idea is important, but “what they may share” is formal and can be easier as a direct question. | “Ask, ‘What will you keep private? What might you tell someone else?’” |

### Quest 6 — Build Mode

No wording was flagged. The support blocks are optional, removable, and capped only for the visual build. The attachment affirmation does not depend on a disclosure or a particular feeling.

### Quest 7 — Safety Power-Ups

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Big mad energy! Give your hands a safe job.” | Trauma therapist | It assumes the child is mad instead of offering a choice that can fit if they are mad. | “Feeling big mad? Pick a safe job for your hands.” |
| “Your body has zoomies. Move it safely.” | Trauma therapist | It assigns a body state and uses a command. | “Body wants to zoom? Pick a safe move.” |
| “You need space. Power words unlocked.” | Trauma therapist | It tells the child what they need. | “Need space? Pick words that can help.” |
| “Something went wrong. Repair is a power move.” | Trauma therapist | It assumes an incident occurred and can create pressure to repair before the child is ready. | “Want a next step? Pick one—or pass.” |
| “You are not bad. Your body needs a mission.” | Trauma therapist | Introducing “bad” may bring shame into the activity, and “needs” is prescriptive. | “Big feelings are okay. Your body can pick a safe job.” |
| “That keeps you and other people safe. Legendary.” | Children’s attorney | A single coping choice cannot guarantee safety in every situation. | “That is one safe move to try. Legendary.” |
| “Safe hands, safe feet, and strong words protect everybody.” | Children’s attorney | “Protect everybody” is an outcome guarantee and may place too much responsibility on the child. | “You can try safe hands, safe feet, or strong words.” |

The urgent line “If you might hurt yourself or someone else, get a safe grown-up now” should stay direct. Its safety value outweighs the longer sentence structure.

### Quest 8 — Pixel Parkour

No wording was flagged. The “boink” response is playful, does not remove XP, and simply repeats the game control.

### Quest 9 — Praise Power-Up

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Shake out yucky feelings.” | Trauma therapist | “Yucky” labels feelings negatively and “shake out” suggests the goal is to remove them. | “Move with big feelings.” |
| “Your body found some power.” | Trauma therapist | The line interprets what happened inside the child after they chose “A lot.” | “You noticed a big change.” |
| “Even a tiny shift counts.” | Reading specialist | “Shift” is abstract and less familiar than “change.” | “A small change counts.” |
| “That’s okay. You still listened to your signal.” | Trauma therapist | It credits a specific internal skill the app cannot observe. | “Not yet is okay. You checked in.” |
| “Opens YouTube Music in a new tab. Brave Blocks cannot see which song you choose or what you do there.” | Reading specialist | The privacy message is useful but long and technical for a child. | “This opens YouTube Music. Brave Blocks cannot see what you play there.” |

The requested question “Did your signal change?” remains appropriate because the screen teaches “signal,” offers “Not yet,” gives equal credit to all three answers, and explicitly says feelings do not have to disappear.

### Quest 10 — Faith Campfire

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I am worth finding.” | Trauma therapist | In an attachment-trauma context, “finding” may evoke being lost, left, or not searched for. | “I matter to God.” |
| “David was small. His courage and God’s help were bigger than his fear.” | Trauma therapist | It can imply that courage or faith should make fear smaller. | “David felt small. God stayed with him while he did a hard thing.” |
| “Hard times can end.” | Children’s attorney | It can be heard as a promise that the child’s current legal or family situation will end in a particular way or soon. | “I can hold on to hope.” |

The faith lines should remain optional and family-led. Nothing in this quest should connect faith, prayer, courage, or “good” behavior to a court or permanency outcome.

### Pause Portal

| Current string | Reviewer | Concern | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I’M READY / GO BACK” | Trauma therapist | The child may want to leave the portal without being ready, and the button should not require claiming readiness. | “GO BACK WHEN YOU WANT →” |

The Pause Portal’s other core lines are strong: “No explaining needed,” “Or just hang here,” “Quiet Cave,” the equal-credit skip, and “Get my grown-up.”

### Loot and completion narration

No additional reward wording was flagged beyond “Support squad: always equipped.” Loot is random and is not tied to a disclosed fact, a particular emotion, truth-telling, agreement, or successful regulation. Passing and skipping receive the same XP and the same chance of loot.

## Strong wording to keep

- “You can say it, point, draw, or pass.”
- “No guessing. No choosing sides.”
- “PASS IS A REAL CHOICE.”
- “You can come back later—or not.”
- “Not ready yet.”
- “Who will you tell?”
- “Skipping is a real move. Still a W.”
- “No explaining needed.”
- “Every answer gets the W. Feelings do not have to disappear.”
- “I don’t know.”
- “If you might hurt yourself or someone else, get a safe grown-up now.”

## Recommended approval order

1. Have the child’s attorney approve or replace the ATTORNEY card and the two privacy-question lines.
2. Have the social worker approve or replace the SOCIAL WORKER card.
3. Have the treating clinician check the Dragon Battle breath change, Safety Power-Ups prompts, and Faith Campfire replacements against the child’s current plan.
4. Have the caregiver and reading specialist read the proposed lines aloud once, listening for rhythm and comprehension.
5. After wording approval, update the visible text and narration source together, generate matching prerecorded clips, run the CHILD vocabulary audit, and test all HEAR IT buttons offline before deployment.

## Narration impact after approval

The wording tables include every flagged prerecorded line or line pattern. Approved changes would require matching new clips for:

- the Quest Host welcome sample;
- all eight Vibe Mixer feeling-and-clue readouts;
- the Dragon Battle HEAR IT/page readout and removal of the timed HOLD cue;
- both professional intro cards;
- all three Talk Power-Up role-play prompts and the shared “Nice move” response;
- the affected Lost Sheep, David and Goliath, and Noah’s Rainbow story/gem readouts.

The remaining page readouts, move labels, Pause Portal readouts, loot announcements, and completion narration were reviewed and did not add another flag. New text should not be deployed until its matching prerecorded clip is present in both the online and offline narration indexes.

## Scope limits

This is a language-risk review, not legal advice, clinical approval, a reading-level diagnosis, or a substitute for the child’s treatment and safety plans. The three reviewer lenses identify plausible concerns for team discussion; they do not claim to speak for the child’s actual therapist, attorney, social worker, or reading specialist.
